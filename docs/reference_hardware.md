# T1 Reference Hardware Specification

**Status:** Proposed (Day 19) — a specification and procurement recommendation,
not a purchase decision. Nothing here has been bought or provisioned.

**Why this exists:** Phase 0.2 called for reference hardware and it was
deferred. Day 18 traced a cascade-bench non-reproduction (4.50% -> 5.10% ->
9.28%) to battery throttling and concurrent load on this project's laptop.
Day 19 built a gate (`src/bench/environment.py`) that refuses to run a
benchmark under those conditions rather than merely detect them after the
fact — and during the writing of *this document*, on *this machine*, that
gate refused four consecutive attempts to certify a cascade-bench artifact:
twice on a stray high-CPU process, and once on a CPU thermally throttled to
24% of full speed *while on AC power and charging*. A laptop cannot be
argued out of this role; it has to be measured out of it, and today's
session did that.

## 1. Why a laptop cannot substitute — the measured evidence

Five cascade-bench readings, one metric, one piece of code, unchanged across
all five:

| # | Value | Day | Environment | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.97% | 2 | `.venv` (unpinned) | verified_drifted |
| 2 | 4.57% | 6 | `.venv-pinned`, quiet AC session | verified_pinned |
| 3 | 4.50% | 17 | `.venv-pinned`, quiet AC session | verified_pinned |
| 4 | 5.10% | 18 | `.venv-pinned`, two concurrent `pytest` suites | not to be quoted |
| 5 | 9.28% | 18 | `.venv-pinned`, battery at 5%, `CPU_Speed_Limit=46` | not to be quoted |

That is a 3.1x spread (2.97% to 9.28%) against a 3.00% product budget, on
identical code, entirely from machine state. A budget this tight cannot be
adjudicated on hardware whose available compute varies by 3x depending on
what else is running and how charged the battery is.

Day 19 adds a sixth and seventh data point, both **refusals** rather than
readings, which is the more important kind of evidence: the new gate would
not let a bad number reach this document at all.

| # | Result | Day | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | REFUSED (start) | 19 | 1 process at/above 20% CPU (`duetexpertd`, a macOS system daemon) |
| 7 | INVALIDATED (mid-run) | 19 | 2 processes at/above 20% CPU appeared during the run |
| 8 | REFUSED (start) | 19 | 1 process at/above 20% CPU (a background helper process) |
| 9 | REFUSED (start) | 19 | `CPU_Speed_Limit=24` **while on AC power, charging at 49%** |

Row 9 is the sharpest finding of the day: Day 18 attributed throttling to a
low, unplugged battery, which reads as a fixable operator error ("just plug
it in"). Row 9 shows the same laptop throttling to a quarter speed *while
plugged in and charging*, mid-session, from sustained CPU load generated by
this project's own test and benchmark runs. AC power is necessary but not
sufficient. There is no operator discipline that fixes a chassis that
cannot dissipate its own sustained load — that is a thermal-envelope
property of the hardware, not a setting.

## 2. Target workload

**8x 720p streams.** Fixed by the Tier-1 sales claim ("a layer of
intelligence on your existing hardware") and by `.claude/skills/
iron-cascade-runtime/SKILL.md`'s budget table, which is the only place this
project has committed to a concrete number.

**Declared fps: currently underspecified.** `scripts/cascade_bench.py`
defaults to 12 fps for its synthetic scenarios, but no config field commits
to a target fps for real camera ingest (`configs/default.yaml` has no `fps`
key). Typical commodity IP cameras run 15-30 fps. This gap should close
before — not after — a reference box is sized, since decoder and stage-0
cost both scale with it. **Flagged as an open item, not resolved here.**

## 3. Required measurements before the Tier-1 claim may be made

None of these exist yet on any hardware. All must be produced *on the
reference box itself*, gated by `src/bench/environment.py`, before "8x720p
within one NUC-class box" is asserted anywhere customer-facing.

1. **Idle cost per camera.** Stage-0 (motion gate) cost as % of one core,
   per camera, with all 8 streams running concurrently — not extrapolated
   from a single-stream measurement, since 8 real decoder/gate threads
   contending for cache and memory bandwidth is not 8x a solo measurement.
2. **Full-pipeline cost at N active tracks.** Stages 1-3 wake on motion;
   the SKILL.md budget table's "5-person scene, full pipeline, 8 cams"
   condition needs an actual N-track load test once stages 1-3 are more
   than stubs (`src/cascade/__init__.py`: "Stages 1-3 are stubs today").
3. **p50/p95/p99 latency**, glass-to-alert, per the two-lane rule (fast
   lane ≤ 800ms p99, scripting ≤ 2.5s p99 per SKILL.md). Mean alone is
   explicitly disallowed by this project's own measurement rules.
4. **Sustained thermal behaviour over 24h.** Not a burst measurement — row
   9 above happened *within one working session* on a laptop with active
   cooling; a fanless or thermally-marginal reference box could throttle
   under real 24/7 load in a way a 20-minute bench run would never surface.
   This is the direct, structural fix for the Day 18/19 finding: measure
   thermal behavior at the timescale the product actually runs at, on the
   box the product actually ships on.

## 4. Minimum viable interim substitute

Buying and burning in dedicated hardware takes weeks. Two credible
stand-ins, with an explicit boundary on what each can and cannot support:

**A — A pinned-CPU cloud instance, no burst credits.**
(e.g. a dedicated-core compute instance, not a burstable/shared-core tier).
*Can support:* relative regression tracking (today vs. last week, on the
same instance type) and thermal-independent correctness/parity checks,
available within hours.
*Cannot support:* the Tier-1 absolute claim. A cloud vCPU is not the CPU
class the product ships on, and "no burst credits" only removes one
confound (credit exhaustion mid-run) — it says nothing about whether the
underlying physical core is shared with a noisy neighbor. Treat any
absolute number from this tier as provisional, re-measured on real
hardware before it is quoted externally.

**B — A desktop on AC with fixed clocks** (turbo/boost disabled in
firmware, plugged into wall power, no battery to throttle in the first
place).
*Can support:* everything a laptop can, more reliably, since the two
confounds Day 18/19 found (battery state, thermal throttling under
sustained laptop-chassis load) are structurally absent from a desktop
with disabled turbo and adequate desktop cooling. A reasonable stand-in
for measurements 1-3 above.
*Cannot support:* measurement 4 (24h sustained thermal behavior) as
evidence for the *actual* reference box, since a desktop tower's thermal
envelope is not a NUC-class box's thermal envelope — the whole reason
row 9 matters is that small, passively- or lightly-cooled form factors are
exactly where sustained-load throttling shows up. Desktop results transfer
for CPU-class regression tracking; they do not transfer for the thermal
claim.

Both interim options are gated by the same `src/bench/environment.py`
machinery — the gate's identity-based `require_comparable` check means an
interim-substitute artifact and a future reference-box artifact will
correctly refuse to compare against each other, rather than silently
implying they are the same measurement.

## 5. Procurement decision memo

**Decision needed:** authorize purchase of a NUC-class reference box (or
equivalent SFF/mini-PC), or approve an interim substitute while procurement
proceeds.

| Option | Approx. cost | Lead time | Unblocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NUC-class reference box** (e.g. Intel NUC 13 Pro-class or equivalent SFF mini-PC, 12-core, 32GB RAM, NVMe, rated for sustained load) | $700-1,200 | 1-3 weeks (retail/OEM order) | All four required measurements (§3), including the 24h thermal test. The only option that actually retires the Tier-1 claim's current "unmeasured" status. |
| **Pinned-CPU cloud instance** | ~$50-150/month | Hours | Relative regression tracking only. Does not unblock the Tier-1 absolute claim or the thermal measurement. |
| **Desktop, fixed clocks, on AC** | $400-800 (or repurpose existing hardware) | Days (if repurposed: none) | Measurements 1-3 (idle cost, full-pipeline cost, latency) as provisional numbers. Does not unblock measurement 4 (thermal) as evidence for the actual reference box. |
| **Status quo (this laptop)** | $0 | None | Nothing. Day 18 and Day 19 together are the evidence this option is exhausted: five wall-clock readings spanning 3.1x, and four consecutive refusals in one session including a throttle event while plugged in and charging. |

**Recommendation:** order the NUC-class reference box now (it is the only
option that unblocks the Tier-1 claim at all), and stand up the desktop
interim (§4-B) in the meantime using whatever AC-powered, fixed-clock
machine is fastest to repurpose, so measurements 1-3 can start accumulating
during the 1-3 week lead time rather than waiting on it idle. Do not
substitute cloud burst instances for anything that will be quoted as an
absolute number.

This is a five-minute decision for whoever holds the budget, not a further
investigation — the evidence for why the current setup cannot answer the
question is already measured, twice, in Day 18 and Day 19.
